  5. In web/clock-core.js, beside the solar functions and exported on ClockCore, with getSunTimes and describeArc left exactly as they are:
  • polarState(date, lat) says why a date has no sunrise or sunset: 'day' when the sun does not set, 'night' when it does not rise, and null when it does both. It uses getSunTimes's own day of year, declination and 90.833° zenith, and compares without dividing — with n = cos(90.833°) − sin(lat)·sin(dec) and d = cos(lat)·cos(dec), 'day' when n < −d and 'night' when n > d — so the poles themselves, where d is zero, answer too;
  • describeDisc(x, y, radius) returns the path of a whole circle (describeArc cannot draw one: a 360° sweep starts and ends on the same point). tests/clock-core.test.js gains: polarState at 90° N is 'day' on 21 June and 'night' on 21 December, and the reverse at 90° S; it is 'night' at 80° N on 21 December, where getSunTimes is null; it is null at 60° N on 21 June and at the equator on both solstices; and describeDisc returns a closed path whose arcs go all the way round. Add both functions' contract lines to docs/planning/solution-plan.md.
  1. In web/index.html a latitude dropdown, #latitude-select, follows the timezone dropdown. Its options, in this order:
  • current, selected at the start: the latitude the daylight is drawn for when nothing else is chosen — the device's under Local Time (or its fallback city's), or the chosen city's. Its label is that latitude rounded to a whole degree with (current) beside it — 41° N (current), 34° S (current), 0° (current) — and it is rewritten whenever that place changes: a location answer, a fallback, a change of timezone. Until a place is known it reads Latitude (current);
  • 90° N, 60° N, 30° N, 0° (Equator), 30° S, 60° S, 90° S. Choosing a fixed latitude replaces only the latitude the daylight is drawn with — in updateSolarGeometry and in updateClock's today wedge — and redraws at once; the longitude, the timezone and the clock's time stay as the timezone dropdown sets them. Choosing current goes back to the place's own. The choice holds across a change of timezone or date, and is not remembered across visits.
  1. Draw the days that have no sunrise or sunset, which 90° N and 90° S always are, and which no place offered before was. Wherever the page gets null from getSolarAngles, it asks polarState:
  • today's daylight (gold) is the whole disc (describeDisc at WEDGE_RADIUS) on a polar day and is cleared on a polar night — it no longer keeps the last place's wedge;
  • the longest day is still picked by length, a polar day counting as the whole day and a polar night as none; its daylight (green) is the whole disc when it is a polar day;
  • the shortest day's markers (blue) are drawn only when that day has a sunrise and a sunset, and are cleared otherwise. The Date override applies as it does now. At 60° and below, and for every city, the face draws exactly as before.
  1. Bump CACHE in web/sw.js to daylight-clock-v7, and update its test in tests/sw.test.js. web/about.html does not change, and tests/architecture.test.js stays as it is: the core gains no DOM and the page calls no server.
